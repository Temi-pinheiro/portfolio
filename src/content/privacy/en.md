## What I collect, and why

### When you visit this site

- **If you allow cookies:** PostHog records the pages you visit, what you click, your browser and device, an approximate location worked out from your IP address, and recordings of your visit. The recordings hide everything you type. The legal basis is your consent, which you give or refuse in the banner.
- **If you say no:** PostHog still counts the visit, without a cookie and without following you from one day to the next. The basis is my legitimate interest in knowing how many people visit and what they read.
- **Server logs:** Netlify, which hosts the site, logs requests, including IP addresses, to serve the pages and keep the site secure. The basis is legitimate interest.

### When you join the email list

Your email address, to send you new editions of The Quiet Edge and news about it. The basis is your consent. Every email has an unsubscribe link. Sendbyte stores the list and sends the emails.

### When you buy something

Whop runs the checkout and takes the payment, under its own privacy policy. It shares with me your name or username, your email address, your country and what you bought. I never see your card details. I use them to deliver what you bought (for the guide, an email with your download link, sent through Sendbyte), to help if something goes wrong, and to keep my accounts. The basis is the contract between us, and my legal duty to keep financial records.

### When you fill in a form, book a call or email me

- The free teardown form asks for your name, email, business, website and what you want to fix. It sends them to my inbox, and I use them to do the teardown and reply.
- Calls are booked through Calendly, which passes me your name, email and whatever you write in the booking.
- If you email me, the conversation stays in my Gmail.

The basis is your request, and my legitimate interest in answering it.

### When I write to you first

Sometimes I write to people who haven’t contacted me, about something tied to their work:

- people who lead HR, training or a team, about a team pack of my guide The Quiet Edge;
- founders whose website or app I think I can improve, about a UI/UX audit;
- writers and creators, about recommending my guide as an affiliate.

For this I use your name, job title, company, work email and, sometimes, a public post or page of yours that made the message relevant. I find them in public sources: company websites, professional profiles, public posts (on Reddit or X, for example) and business contact databases such as Apollo. My first message says where I found your contact.

I send a first message and at most two short follow-ups, then stop. Reply “stop”, or anything like it, and I won’t write again. I then keep only your email address on a do-not-contact list, so I never write to you by mistake.

The basis is my legitimate interest in offering relevant work to businesses, weighed against yours: work contact details only, an offer related to your job, few messages, and a way out in every one. You can object at any time.

### AI tools

I use AI assistants, currently Anthropic’s Claude, to help research, write and send some of this work, including finding business contacts and drafting emails. They process the data above to do that work for me.

### What I don’t do

I don’t sell personal data, I don’t run ads, and I don’t share your data with anyone other than the services named in this notice.

## How long I keep it

- **Analytics:** as long as PostHog’s plan keeps it: currently a year for events and a month for recordings.
- **Email list:** until you unsubscribe. After that, only your address, marked as unsubscribed, so you’re never added again by mistake.
- **Purchases:** as long as tax and accounting law requires, usually six years.
- **Forms and emails:** while they’re useful for the work, and no more than two years after our last exchange unless we’re working together.
- **Outreach with no reply:** deleted within 12 months of my last message. The do-not-contact list is kept for as long as I send outreach.
